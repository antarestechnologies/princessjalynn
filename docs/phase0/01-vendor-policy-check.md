## 1. Summary

Across the five categories, only a handful of vendors have policy text that **affirmatively contemplates legal adult content**: **bunny.net** (CDN, Storage and Stream — its ToS/FAQ, per search excerpt, say it accepts legal adult content), **DigitalOcean** (Droplets, App Platform, Managed Postgres, Spaces — ToS says it "may permit, in its sole discretion, adult websites that abide by state and federal law and regulation"), and **Twilio SendGrid** (whose prohibited-content article, per excerpt, says an adult *business* can send non-explicit email with normal deliverability, while explicit content in the email body is banned). Several large infrastructure providers are **silent** — they ban CSAM and non-consensual imagery but say nothing about lawful adult material: **Backblaze B2**, **AWS** (AUP dropped the word "obscene" in 2021), **Google Cloud** (read directly), **Cloudflare** (Stream/R2; the old "non-HTML" clause 2.8 was removed in 2023 and replaced by a CDN-only clause that is satisfied when media is hosted on Stream/Images/R2), **Crunchy Bridge**, **Supabase**, **Neon**, **Railway**, **Mux**, **api.video**, **Gcore**, **VdoCipher**, **Wasabi**, **PlanetScale** — these are **UNCONFIRMED** and need a one-line email to sales/support. **Explicitly prohibited or effectively unusable:** **Hetzner** ("erotic, pornographic … not permitted"), **Fly.io** (Customer Data "shall not … be … obscene, pornographic"), **Vimeo/Vimeo OTT** (no pornography or sexually explicit content), **Brevo** (pornography listed as indecent/prohibited), and for *explicit* email content **Resend** and **Postmark** ("Pornography/sexually explicit content" on their prohibited lists; non-explicit transactional mail for an adult business is unaddressed, so UNCONFIRMED). **Vercel** bans "obscene, graphic … sexually exploitative" content without defining it and never mentions lawful adult content — UNCONFIRMED and, in my judgment, the highest-risk mainstream choice because the wording is broad and enforcement is discretionary. **Azure** is contradictory (the Product Terms AUP is silent, but Microsoft's Digital Safety policy for "Microsoft's products and services" bans nudity/pornography) — UNCONFIRMED, high risk.

## 3. Recommended shortlist (~120 subs, ~200 GB video, ~2 TB/mo egress)

Prices below are **approximate, from general knowledge, not re-verified this session** (search budget exhausted). Treat them as order-of-magnitude.

**App hosting**
1. **DigitalOcean Droplet or App Platform** — the only mainstream platform whose terms explicitly contemplate legal adult sites, and it keeps DB + object storage under one vendor and one AUP. A $12–24/mo Droplet (or $5–12/mo App Platform component) is more than enough for 120 subscribers. Get the "sole discretion" confirmation in writing via sales first.
2. **AWS (ECS/Fargate or Lightsail)** — fallback; no prohibition in the current AUP, large adult platforms are known to run on it, but silent, so email first. ~$15–40/mo at this scale.
   *Avoid:* Vercel (broad "obscene/graphic" ban, discretionary), Fly.io and Hetzner (explicit bans), Azure (contradictory policies).

**Postgres**
1. **DigitalOcean Managed Postgres** — same vendor umbrella as hosting; from ~$15/mo (1 GB) — ample.
2. **Crunchy Bridge** — AUP is purely legality-based (no "obscene"/"offensive" language surfaced); Postgres specialists; ~$35/mo entry. Email to confirm.
   Alternative: self-host Postgres on the Droplet (zero policy exposure, you own backups).

**Video**
1. **Bunny Stream** — explicit adult-OK text, token-authenticated playback, built-in transcoding, cheap: storage ≈ $0.01/GB/mo (200 GB ≈ $2), delivery ≈ $0.005–0.01/GB (2 TB ≈ $10–20). Realistic bill: **$15–30/mo**.
2. **Cloudflare Stream** — no prohibition found and widely used, but silent; priced per minute ($5/1,000 min stored, $1/1,000 min delivered) which for ~100 h stored and ~2 TB delivered lands around **$100–150/mo** — far pricier than Bunny at this scale. Only pick if Cloudflare confirms in writing.
   *Avoid:* Vimeo OTT (prohibited).

**Transactional email**
1. **Twilio SendGrid** — the only mainstream provider whose published guidance explicitly says an adult business may send (non-explicit) email with normal deliverability. Free tier 100/day or Essentials ≈ $20/mo; 120 subscribers need far less. Keep every email text-only, no imagery, neutral sender name.
2. **Amazon SES** — ~$0.10 per 1,000 emails; requires production-access request where you describe the use case — do that honestly and get the approval on record. UNCONFIRMED until then.
   *Avoid for now:* Resend, Postmark (pornography on prohibited lists; would need written exception), Brevo (prohibited).

**CDN / object storage (thumbnails, previews, downloads, backups)**
1. **Bunny Storage + Bunny CDN** — explicit adult-OK text; storage ≈ $0.01–0.02/GB/mo, CDN ≈ $0.01/GB (NA/EU). 200 GB + 2 TB ≈ **$25/mo** all-in; pairs naturally with Bunny Stream.
2. **Backblaze B2** (≈ $6/TB/mo, so 200 GB ≈ $1.20; egress free up to 3× stored data per month, then $0.01/GB; free egress to Cloudflare/Bunny via Bandwidth Alliance) — cheapest durable store and master backup; no adult prohibition in its AUP, but silent, so email. Fronting B2 with Bunny CDN gives a fully adult-permitted delivery path.

**Suggested baseline stack (all PERMITTED or no-prohibition-found):** DigitalOcean (app + managed Postgres + Spaces) · Bunny Stream + Bunny CDN/Storage · Backblaze B2 for cold backup · SendGrid for transactional email. Rough total at this scale: **$80–130/mo**.
