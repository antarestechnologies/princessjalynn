## 1. Summary

- **Pick an IPSP (payment facilitator), not a gateway.** For a single-creator site doing ~$1,800/month, CCBill, Segpay, Epoch, Verotel and Vendo all let you process under *their* master merchant account; you do not need your own acquiring bank relationship. NETbilling and RocketGate are gateways that assume you already have (or will get) a high-risk merchant account, which at this volume adds cost, a personal guarantee, and weeks of underwriting. Paxum is a payout e-wallet, not a way for fans to pay by card.
- **Hosted checkout is available from every IPSP candidate** (CCBill FlexForms, Segpay hosted pay page, Epoch hosted join page, Verotel FlexPay order pages, Vendo Payment Links / hosted pages), so the "no card data on our servers" rule is satisfiable with any of them.
- **Published pricing exists only for CCBill, Epoch and Verotel.** Segpay, Vendo, NETbilling and RocketGate are quote-only. Reported adult-tier rates cluster at **~11–15.5% of gross** plus **$1,450–$1,950/yr in Visa/Mastercard high-risk registration fees** passed through, plus a **5–10% rolling reserve for 6 months** at most of them.
- **Honest fee picture:** at $1,800/month gross, the all-in processor cost lands at roughly **18–25% of gross**, i.e. *at or above* OnlyFans' 20%, before hosting, video, ID-verification and legal costs. The case for leaving OnlyFans is ownership of the fan list, control over pricing/content policy, and platform risk — not a lower fee at this scale. See §6.
- **Application blockers Carson controls:** an LLC + EIN + business bank account, a live/staging site with every legal page present, performer ID + 2257 records, and a documented content-review and complaint process (card-brand rules). Start the LLC and attorney work first; processors will not approve a site with placeholder legal pages.

## 3. Master application checklist

Check each item before applying. Items marked **(attorney)** need the attorney's copy or sign-off per PLAN.md non-negotiable #7.

### A. Business
- [ ] **Entity formed** (LLC recommended over sole proprietorship: separates performer's legal name from merchant records and billing descriptor). Articles of organization / certificate of formation on hand.
- [ ] **EIN letter** (IRS CP-575) — processors ask for it even for single-member LLCs.
- [ ] **Operating agreement** naming owner(s) — some underwriters request it to confirm beneficial ownership (KYC/AML).
- [ ] **Owner government ID** (passport or driver's license), unexpired, both sides; plus **proof of address** (utility bill/bank statement < 90 days).
- [ ] **Beneficial-ownership disclosure**: anyone with ≥25% ownership will be asked for ID. If the performer is a co-owner, she is identified here *and* under section B.
- [ ] **Business description** (one paragraph): "subscription membership site featuring content from a single verified adult performer; pay-per-view unlocks and tips; no user-generated content; no live cams" — the "no UGC" point materially simplifies card-brand review.
- [ ] **Projected volume**: monthly gross (~$1,800), average ticket ($15 subscription; PPV range; tip range), transaction count (~150–200/mo), % recurring vs one-time. Vendo asks for 6 months of history if any exists (OnlyFans statements can serve as evidence of real demand — confirm with each processor whether they accept them).
- [ ] **Prior processing history**: any terminated accounts or MATCH listing (none expected). Be ready to state "none".
- [ ] **Customer-support email and phone** (business line or VoIP number, monitored). Several processors require a phone number on the site.
- [ ] **Billing descriptor decided** (see Site & legal, below).

### B. Performer & 18 U.S.C. §2257
- [ ] **Performer government ID** (unexpired; ID copy retained per 28 C.F.R. Part 75). Card-brand rules require "verifying the age and identity of content providers using government-issued ID" (Mastercard AN 5196; Visa rule 0003356 as reported by Segpay).
- [ ] **Model release / performer agreement** **(attorney)**: written agreement between the LLC and the performer that (a) grants distribution rights, (b) confirms consent to public distribution, (c) confirms all content is legal, (d) sets the takedown/appeal process. Visa's rule, as reported: "The merchant must enter into a written agreement with the content provider and the agreement must include that the content does not have any activity that is illegal." Even though performer and owner overlap, processors want the document.
- [ ] **Consent documentation per shoot/series** **(attorney)**: Visa (per Segpay's summary) requires records confirming "consent given to be depicted, with proof that consent was given to allow public distribution of the content." Design the 2257 vault (PLAN Phase 5) to store a consent record per media item or per session.
- [ ] **2257 records set** for the performer: legal name, date of birth, all stage names/aliases, copy of ID, and a cross-index from every published work to that record (18 U.S.C. §2257(b); 28 C.F.R. §75.2). **(attorney to confirm who the "producer" is — the LLC, the performer, or both — and whether any §2257A/exemption applies.)**
- [ ] **Custodian of records named**: a real person, with a real business address (not a P.O. box — **(attorney)** to confirm acceptable address type). Statute: "if the person to whom subsection (a) … applies is an organization the statement … shall include the name, title, and business address of the individual employed by such organization responsible for maintaining the records."
- [ ] **2257 compliance statement page** drafted **(attorney)**, and the statute's placement rule understood: the statement must be "affixed to every copy" and "the term 'copy' includes every page of a website on which matter described in subsection (a) appears." Implement as a site-wide footer link to the statement page, plus the statement on the page itself (confirm with attorney whether a link satisfies 28 C.F.R. §75.8).
- [ ] **Content-review-before-publish process documented** (one page): who reviews, what they check (legality, performer identity matches 2257 record, no third parties), where approval is logged. Mastercard AN 5196: "All uploaded content must be reviewed before publication." Enforce in software (PLAN Phase 5: "a media item cannot be published without a linked 2257 record").
- [ ] **Complaint / takedown process documented** and reachable from every page: Mastercard requires "a complaint process that allows for the reporting of content that may be illegal or otherwise violates the Standards" and the "ability for any person depicted … to appeal to remove such content." Define SLA (e.g., review within 7 days) **(attorney)**.
- [ ] **Monthly reporting readiness**: Visa program (as reported) requires merchants to "submit monthly reports on their activity to their acquirer." Ask each processor what they need from a single-performer, no-UGC site — often a short attestation.

### C. Site & legal pages (live or on a staging URL the processor can open)
- [ ] **Age gate / 18+ interstitial** before any content, including thumbnails (PLAN non-negotiable #4). Processors explicitly list "18+ gates" as a requirement.
- [ ] **Working signup → checkout → member-area flow** on staging, pointed at the processor's test mode. Underwriters click through it.
- [ ] **Terms of Service** **(attorney)** — include: age requirement, license to content (no download/redistribution), account termination, governing law, arbitration if desired.
- [ ] **Privacy Policy** **(attorney)** — covers payment data (processed by the processor, not by us), ID-verification vendor data flow, cookies, data retention, deletion requests (PLAN Phase 6).
- [ ] **Refund & Cancellation Policy** **(attorney)** — plain language: how to cancel (self-serve link + processor's own cancel/support URL, e.g. CCBill/Segpay/Epoch consumer support pages), when renewals bill, what is refundable. Card brands require express consent for recurring billing: "express consent must be obtained via a 'click to accept button' on the checkout screen" and clear disclosure of renewal terms. Show price, billing frequency, and cancellation method on the checkout page itself, not just in the ToS.
- [ ] **2257 statement page** (from section B).
- [ ] **DMCA / Contact page** **(attorney)** with DMCA designated agent registered at the Copyright Office (fee applies), plus the content-complaint/takedown form (section B). Processors list "complaints policy" explicitly.
- [ ] **Customer support contact** (email + phone) visible in footer and on checkout. Required by underwriters and by card-brand descriptor rules.
- [ ] **Pricing clearly displayed** on the join page before checkout: subscription price, renewal term, PPV prices, tip ranges, currency. No hidden trial-to-paid conversions.
- [ ] **Cross-sale / upsell disclosure**: if you ever offer a second product at checkout, it must be opt-in with its own clear price. CCBill: cross-sales "require a pre-approved Cross Sale Relationship between partners" and are limited in number/placement; FTC negative-option rules apply. Recommendation: **no cross-sales at launch**; state that in the application.
- [ ] **Billing descriptor chosen and shown to the fan before purchase** ("This charge will appear on your statement as ___"). Under the IPSP model the descriptor is largely the processor's brand (e.g., CCBill.com / Segpay / Epoch / Verotel) followed by a short merchant/site token — which is itself discreet. Ask each processor: what exactly appears, how many characters you control, and whether a neutral LLC name can be used instead of the site name. Mismatched descriptors are a top cause of "I don't recognize this charge" chargebacks.
- [ ] **No third-party or user-generated content** anywhere on the site (keeps you out of the heavier UGC-platform rules).
- [ ] **No prohibited content categories** — review the processor's AUP (each publishes one) and card-brand standards; attorney reviews content policy.
- [ ] **Robots/indexing**: media and member pages not indexable (PLAN Phase 2) — underwriters sometimes Google the domain.
- [ ] **Watermark/anti-leak statement** optional but helpful: shows the processor you are protecting the performer (lower takedown/complaint risk).

### D. Banking
- [ ] **Business bank account** in the LLC's name (adult-friendly bank; confirm the bank's own policy before depositing processor payouts — some banks close accounts over "adult" descriptors on incoming wires).
- [ ] **Voided check or bank letter** showing account name, routing and account numbers; some processors accept a recent statement header.
- [ ] **Payout method chosen**: ACH (cheapest at CCBill $5) vs wire (Verotel $30/wk USD) vs Paxum/Payoneer (Segpay, Epoch). Set minimum payout to batch weekly payouts into fewer, cheaper transfers if fees are per-payout.
- [ ] **Reserve cash-flow plan**: expect 5% (CCBill/Segpay/Vendo) to 10% (Verotel) of gross withheld for 8–26 weeks. At $1,800/mo that is $90–$180/mo withheld, up to ~$540–$1,170 floating before it begins returning.
- [ ] **Registration fee cash**: $1,450–$1,950 may be due **up front** (Vendo: "paid in advance for the first time"; Epoch: "$1,950 initial").
- [ ] **W-9 / tax**: processors issue 1099-K; keep EIN consistent across application, bank, and W-9.

### E. Card-brand & risk
- [ ] Read and keep: **Mastercard AN 5196** "Revised Standards for New Specialty Merchant Registration Requirements for Adult Content Merchants" (published 2021-04-13, effective 2021-10-15) — PDF mirrored by Segpay: https://segpay.com/MC_RevisedStandardsForNewSpecialtyMerchantRegistrationRequirementsForAdultContentMerchants.pdf
- [ ] Read: Segpay's summary of **Visa's adult-content rule (Visa Rule ID 0003356, Global Brand Protection Program → now Visa Integrity Risk Program)**: https://segpay.com/blog/visa-unveils-new-regulations-for-content-providers/
- [ ] Confirm with the processor **which MCC** they will register you under (commonly **5967** Direct Marketing – Inbound Teleservices for digital adult content) — you do not pick it, but it drives the registration fees and the Visa Integrity Risk per-transaction fee.
- [ ] Budget **Visa $950 + Mastercard $500 per year** registration (see §4) and, where applied, **Visa Integrity Risk fee ~$0.10 + 10 bps per transaction**.
- [ ] **Chargeback playbook written** before launch (see §4): refund-first policy, response SLA, how the admin revenue view (PLAN Phase 4) surfaces the ratio weekly.
- [ ] Ask each processor whether they enroll merchants in **Visa RDR / Ethoca / Verifi alerts** (pre-dispute refunds that keep disputes out of your ratio) and at what cost.
- [ ] **Fraud controls** on by default: AVS/CVV, velocity limits, 3-D Secure where offered, block obviously mismatched geography. Processors include basic screening; ask what is on.

## 4. Card-brand compliance notes for a solo creator

### 4.1 High-risk registration (the fixed cost nobody mentions in the headline rate)
- Visa raised its high-risk merchant registration fee from **$500 to $950 per year** effective **April 1, 2024**, as part of the **Visa Integrity Risk Program (VIRP)** that replaced the Global Brand Protection Program. Mastercard's registration fee **remains $500 per year** (Mastercard Registration Program, MRP). Sources: PaymentCloud, Host Merchant Services, Vendo blog, Segpay blog (links in §7).
- VIRP puts **adult content in Tier 1** (highest integrity risk) alongside dating/escort, gambling, pharmacies. Acquirers pay their own $100k tier fees; merchants pay the $950 and, for certain MCCs including 5967, an **Integrity Risk fee of $0.10 per transaction + 10 basis points** of volume (as reported by Segpay and others — confirm whether your processor passes this through).
- Under the IPSP model these fees are **passed through** (CCBill, Segpay, Vendo, Epoch all report charging them). CCBill/Segpay report $950 + $500 = **$1,450/yr**; Vendo reports **€1,450/yr**; Epoch reports **$1,950** initial and annual. Verotel's published plan does not itemize them — ask.
- Non-compliance fines under VIRP can reach very large sums (reported "up to $400,000" at the acquirer level), which is *why* processors underwrite adult merchants so carefully and why your compliance pages must be real before you apply.

### 4.2 Chargeback programs — where the "1%" danger zone comes from
- **Visa VAMP (Visa Acquirer Monitoring Program)** replaced VDMP/VFMP on **April 1, 2025**. Ratio = (TC40 fraud reports + TC15 disputes) ÷ settled transactions. The merchant "Excessive" threshold dropped to **1.5% on April 1, 2026** (from 2.2%), with **$8 per flagged transaction** fines and no warning tier. A merchant must also exceed a **minimum of ~1,500 combined fraud+dispute events in a month** to be enrolled — at ~200 transactions/month you cannot hit that count, so VAMP enforcement is unlikely to touch you directly. **But**: your *processor's* portfolio ratio is what Visa measures at the acquirer level, so IPSPs enforce their *own* internal cutoffs, historically around **1%**, and will terminate or reserve-up merchants who exceed it regardless of count. Disputes resolved through **RDR/CDRN pre-dispute** are excluded from the ratio; fraud TC40s that later become TC15 disputes **count twice**.
- **Mastercard ECP (Excessive Chargeback Program)**: **ECM** = ≥100 chargebacks in a month *and* ratio 1.5%–2.99%; **HECM** = ≥300 chargebacks *and* ≥3%. Fines escalate from ~$1,000/month to $200,000+, plus possible **MATCH** listing (industry blacklist that follows the owner personally for 5 years). Again, the count floor means Mastercard itself will not flag you at 200 transactions/month, but your processor will.
- **Practical rule for Carson:** treat **0.5% as yellow and 1.0% as red** on your own dashboard (that is 1 and 2 chargebacks per month at ~200 transactions). One bad month can end the account.

### 4.3 Chargeback vs refund — why refund fast
- A **refund** is initiated by you; you lose the sale and the processing fee (Epoch charges $1 per credit; Segpay $0.05; CCBill reports $0). It does **not** count in any card-brand ratio.
- A **chargeback** is initiated by the cardholder's bank; funds are pulled, a fee is assessed ($12.50 Epoch, $25 CCBill on most plans, typically $15–$25 industry-wide), and it **counts against your ratio even if you win the dispute**. In adult, "friendly fraud" (a partner sees the statement) is the dominant dispute reason, and the merchant almost never wins.
- Therefore: **refund any unhappy fan immediately and without argument**, publish a generous cancel/refund policy, send renewal reminder emails before each rebill, make the cancel link one click, and enroll in pre-dispute alerts (Ethoca/Verifi/RDR) so you can refund inside the ~24-hour window before a dispute becomes a chargeback. The admin revenue view in PLAN Phase 4 should show refunds and chargebacks as separate counters with the ratio computed weekly.

### 4.4 Content-rule obligations (AN 5196 / Visa 0003356) restated as a build list
1. Age + identity of every performer verified against government ID, records retained (2257 vault).
2. Written agreement with the content provider confirming legality and consent to distribute.
3. Documented consent of every person depicted, including consent to *public distribution*.
4. **Every item reviewed before publication**; review logged (audit_log).
5. Complaint/report mechanism on the site; defined review/takedown SLA.
6. Right of any depicted person to request removal, with a documented appeal path.
7. Ongoing monitoring and ability to take content down quickly.
8. Monthly activity attestation to the processor if requested.

For a single-performer, no-UGC site, items 1–3 collapse to one performer file plus per-shoot consent records, and 4 is a one-person approval step — but they still have to be *written down* and *enforced in software*, because the processor will ask to see the process.

## 5. Recommended pre-application sequence

1. **Form the LLC → get EIN → open a business bank account** at a bank confirmed to accept adult-industry payouts. (Weeks 1–3; name the LLC something neutral so it can double as a discreet descriptor if the processor allows.)
2. **Engage the attorney** for: ToS, Privacy, Refund/Cancel, 2257 statement + custodian designation, performer agreement/model release, DMCA agent registration, content-review and complaint SOPs. Ask the attorney to confirm the "producer" analysis under §2257 and the state age-verification obligations (PLAN Phase 0 item 3). (Weeks 1–6, in parallel.)
3. **Build the site to staging with the fake `PaymentProcessor`** (PLAN Phase 4) so that signup → checkout → member area → cancel all work end to end, age gate on, legal pages present (placeholder text clearly marked until attorney copy lands). Underwriters need to click through a working flow; they do **not** need live payments.
4. **Assemble the KYC packet** (section A/B/D above) into one folder: entity docs, EIN, owner ID + proof of address, performer ID, performer agreement, voided check, projected volumes, OnlyFans earnings statements as demand evidence.
5. **Swap placeholders for attorney copy** on ToS/Privacy/Refund/2257/DMCA. Do not apply with "[ATTORNEY COPY NEEDED]" visible.
6. **Apply to two IPSPs in parallel** — suggested pair: **CCBill** (largest, published adult pricing, FlexForms + webhooks, US-based) and **Segpay** (US-based IPSP, weekly Tuesday payouts, strong postback docs and test mode). Keep **Epoch** as the fallback (no reserve, but 15% at your tier and $1,950 registration). Consider **Vendo** only if you want EU-acquirer diversification later; **Verotel** is EU-centric with a 10%/26-week hold and $30 USD wire fees — weak fit for a US creator at this volume.
7. **Negotiate before signing**: ask each for (a) the exact percentage, (b) registration-fee pass-through amount and timing, (c) reserve % and duration, (d) payout cadence/fees/minimum, (e) chargeback fee and their internal ratio cutoff, (f) descriptor options, (g) RDR/alert availability, (h) sandbox credentials now.
8. **Integrate the one that approves first** behind the `PaymentProcessor` interface: hosted checkout redirect, signed webhook/postback handler (idempotent by event ID), cancel/refund calls. Keep the second application warm as a backup processor; adult merchants are routinely advised to maintain two.
9. **Pre-launch**: processor reviews the *live* site (PLAN Phase 6 "Done when … the processor has approved the live site"). Turn off test mode only after their written go-live.
10. **Post-launch**: watch the chargeback counter weekly; refund fast; keep 2257/consent records current for every new upload.

## 6. Honest fee comparison vs OnlyFans (at ~120 × $15 = $1,800/month gross)

OnlyFans keeps **20%** of all creator earnings (subscriptions, PPV, tips) and pays 80% [T; OnlyFans ToS §"Platform Fee on Creator Earnings" as mirrored by ConductAtlas]. At $1,800/month that is **$360/month, $4,320/year**, and it includes processing, hosting, video delivery, support and card-brand compliance.

Assumptions below: ~160 transactions/month (120 renewals + ~40 PPV/tips); ACH or wire payout weekly; figures are the *reported* ones from §2 and are **estimates until quoted**.

| Cost line | CCBill (adult, reported) | Epoch (reported) | Verotel Basic (reported) | Segpay / Vendo |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Processing % | 10.8%–14.5% → **$194–$261** | 15.0% (tier ≤$5k) → **$270** | 15.5% + 1.5% recurring → **~$306** | quote-only |
| Per-txn fees | not reported | $1/refund, $12.50/CB (variable) | none reported | Segpay $0.10/auth → ~$16 |
| Card-brand registration (annualized) | $1,450/yr → **$121** | $1,950/yr → **$163** | not itemized **[?]** | $1,450–€1,450/yr → ~$121–$130 |
| Payout fees | ACH $5/wk → **~$22** | **[?]** | USD wire $30/wk → **~$130** (or batch monthly) | wire/ACH/Paxum **[?]** |
| **Approx. monthly total** | **$337–$404 (18.7%–22.4%)** | **~$433+ (24%)** | **~$436+ (24%)** (less if batching payouts) | **[?]** likely in the same band |
| Reserve (cash-flow, not a cost) | 5% for 8–26 wks → $90/mo withheld, $180–$540 float | none | 10% for 26 wks → $180/mo withheld, ~$1,080 float | 5% for 6 months → ~$540 float |

**Then add what OnlyFans was absorbing:** hosting + database (est. $20–$60/mo), video encoding/delivery with signed URLs and watermarking (est. $20–$100/mo at this scale, vendor-dependent — see Phase 0 DRM check), transactional email (~$0–$20), ID/age-verification per new signup (est. $1–$3 per check, vendor quotes pending), domain/DMCA agent/annual LLC fees (~$15–$40/mo amortized), and one-time attorney fees (unknown; budget seriously). **All of those estimates are mine, not vendor quotes.**

**Bottom line:** at $1,800/month the self-hosted route costs roughly **the same as or more than OnlyFans' 20%** — realistically **22–30% of gross all-in** in year one once registration fees, payout fees, infrastructure and ID verification are included, plus the legal setup cost. The fee math improves with scale (Epoch's tiers and CCBill's lower band kick in well above $5k/month; the $1,450–$1,950 registration becomes a smaller share) and with annual/longer subscriptions (fewer transactions). The real reasons to proceed are **ownership of the subscriber list and email addresses, control over pricing and content policy, independence from a single platform's account-termination risk, and the ability to run PPV/tips without a platform cap** — not a fee saving at today's volume. Carson should decide with that framing.
